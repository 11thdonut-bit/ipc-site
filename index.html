Terran Operations

Modern infrastructure is still certified with decades-old equipment.

Infrastructure is being built at a rate the industry has not seen in two generations: data centers, transmission, water, highways, roads, bridges, and more. The equipment certifying the ground under all of it is decades old. Analog, slow, imprecise enough that two technicians on the same lift return different answers, and still built around a sealed radioactive source, with the federal license, locked cage and badged operator that come with it.

SPARTA brings 21st century, space-grade hardware to that problem: more properties per insertion, in minutes rather than days, by one person, with no radioactive source to license, store or transport.

Tap the Moon or the Earth

Prospecting is an old word. It used to mean walking out with a pick and finding out for yourself what the ground was made of. We would like to keep doing that, a bit further out.

Inside this decade, national agencies and commercial operators intend to put landers, power plants, habitats and mining equipment on the lunar south pole. Dozens of touchdowns on a few square kilometers of permanently shadowed ground. They are going for water ice to make propellant, for regolith to build and shield with, and for a surface flat and firm enough to land on reliably.

Almost everything known about the surfaces of alien worlds, including the Moon, is inferred from orbit. Bearing strength, thermal behavior, how much ice is actually in it and in what form: none of it can be settled from a photograph. Somebody has to put an instrument into the surface and measure it.

That is where we sit. Interplanetary Prospecting Corporation was founded in 2026 in Pasadena, California, and we build field instruments, flight instruments, and the surveys that run with them.

We are the frontier of geophysical exploration for the Earth, the Moon and beyond.

SPARTA

SPARTA is our first instrument. One probe goes into the ground once and comes back with four independent measurements of it: how hard it is, how it fails, how it moves heat, and how much water is in it.

The underlying sensing was developed at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory. What are building now is a separate design derived from that work and re-engineered for terrestrial use: ordinary sites, ordinary crews, a result in minutes, and no radioactive source to license, store or transport.

  • Penetration resistanceBearing capacity, compaction and relative density, indexed against depth.
  • Shear strengthMeasured in place, without pulling a sample or disturbing the ground being measured.
  • Thermal conductivityHow the ground moves heat, across the range a working site actually sees.
  • Dielectric responseWater and ice content, resolved with no radioactive source and no licensed operator.
